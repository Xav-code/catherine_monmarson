The path

Born 1947 in Orléans. Beaux-Arts de Paris. South of France since 1974 — Châteauneuf-de-Grasse, then Biot from 2012, where she also leads visual arts workshops. Around twenty-five solo and group shows between 1993 and 2015, in France, Belgium and Denmark.

Known gaps

55 works still carry a camera filename instead of a title. Roughly 40% have no year. The sculptures have height and width but no depth. And there is exactly one photograph per work — no second angle, no surface detail, no scale shot. All of it is fixable, and all of it needs Cathy (§06).

01Structure

One site, two languages — French by default, English second. French is her market; English is her reach. Five destinations a visitor navigates, plus two they are sent to.

Deliberately absent: a sidebar, a homepage carousel, social-proof badges, a “trending” rail. The restraint is the brand.

02Design system

No accent colour anywhere. The paintings are the colour; the page gives them a warm paper ground and one hairline rule, and otherwise stays out of the way.

TokenDecision
Ground #FAF9F7 — warm paper white. Never pure white behind pigment.
Ink #1A1A1A, muted #6B6560
Accent None. One hairline rule at #E4E0DA.
Display type Cormorant Garamond — titles, prices, dimensions.
Text type Inter, 15–17 px, 1.7 line-height.
Rhythm One work per viewport in editorial sections; 2-up or 3-up only in the index grid.
Motion Opacity-only fade, 400 ms. No parallax, no zoom-on-hover.
Dark mode Token-swapped — but artwork always sits on a near-paper plate, never inverted.

Accessibility throughout: AA contrast, visible focus rings, a full keyboard path through the lightbox and the filters, and real alt text per work built from its medium and subject.

03The artwork page

This is where sales happen, so it gets the most thought. Seven parts, in order down the page.

  1. Image stage. Full-height, click to zoom to HD, arrow keys between works.
  2. Angles strip. For sculpture: front, three-quarter, profile, detail — thumbnails under the stage.
  3. Room preview. The work at true scale on a 3,20 m wall, with a 1,75 m silhouette you can toggle. Sculpture stands on a 100 cm plinth. The single biggest conversion lever for ceramic and for large canvas.
  4. Facts. Title, year, medium verbatim (“Pigments sur toile”, “Terre enfumée, raku”), height × width in cm and inches, unique, signed, certificate of authenticity.
  5. Price and action. Up to €800, a direct Acheter. Above that, Acquérir — a form, answered within 48 hours. Sold: “demander une œuvre proche de cette série”.
  6. Reassurance. Ships from Biot, insured, packed to measure; crated freight for sculpture; 14-day return.
  7. Context. Three further works from the same series. Nothing else.

04Commerce

Launch on Stripe Payment Links. No shop platform.

Every work is unique, quantity one. Stripe gives card plus Apple and Google Pay and French VAT handling with no monthly fee — about 1,5% + €0,25 on EU cards. The enquiry form carries everything above €800, because at €1 500–4 800 fine art genuinely sells by conversation, and a button that opens a dialogue converts better than a cart.

What is already wired

Each work decides its own button from its price and availability. A direct Acheter appears only once a Stripe Payment Link exists for that work; until then it routes to the enquiry form, pre-filled with which piece it is about. Adding a link is pasting one URL into a file and rebuilding — no keys, no server, nothing secret in the repository.

If volume later justifies it

Shopify Basic at roughly €29/month, sitting behind the same custom front-end through the Storefront API — real inventory, shipping zones, a tax engine, abandoned-cart email. Not before it is needed.

Why not a Squarespace or Shopify theme outright

A template gets 80% of this look in a weekend. But the one-work-per-screen stage, the room preview and the sculpture angle strip all fight the template. A custom static site costs the same to host — nothing — and is strictly faster.

To settle with Cathy

Shipping tiers for France, the EU and worldwide. A rolled-in-tube option for unstretched canvas. Crated freight quoted individually for sculpture and for canvases over 130 cm.

06What Cathy owes the site

This is the final 20% of quality, and none of it can be invented. In priority order:

  1. Titles and years for the 55 works currently reading P10154xx.JPG. One pass down a list.
  2. Depth for the sculptures — height and width came through from ArtMajeur, depth did not. Plus a weight bracket, for crating.
  3. A second photograph of each sculpture (three-quarter view) and one detail of the surface.
  4. Studio photographs — six to eight frames: hands in clay, a firing, the reed and branch tools, pigment jars, a wall of work in progress. This is the entire Singulart layer.
  5. A 400-word statement in her own voice, in French, first person. The ArtMajeur text is third-person; it needs to become hers.
  6. A 60–90 second video of a raku firing. Nothing sells ceramic like opening a kiln.
  7. A confirmed price list — current euros, and which works are genuinely still available.
  8. Legal — SIRET and artist-author status, mentions légales, CGV, the GDPR text.

08Visibility beyond the site

The site is the asset. These are the pumps that fill it.

  • SEO. Per-work VisualArtwork structured data, French long-tail — “sculpture raku Côte d'Azur”, “peintre figure humaine Biot” — and a Google Business Profile for the studio.
  • Instagram. One work per post, caption in French and English, linking to that work's own URL. Reels of firings.
  • Newsletter. Monthly, one work and one paragraph. Buttondown, free under 100 subscribers.
  • Local. Biot, Valbonne and Antibes tourism listings, the Côte d'Azur gallery circuit, open-studio weekends, and the Lions Club and Festival d'Art de Seillans contacts she already has.
  • Press kit. One PDF — portrait, six works, statement, CV, contact. Nice-Matin already covered her in 2003.
  • Marketplaces stay. ArtMajeur, Singulart and Saatchi become discovery funnels pointing home.
